# Restaurant-Style Gambas al Ajillo with Lemon and Parsley

Shell-on shrimp sizzle in a shallow garlic-infused oil until their shells turn coral and the flesh turns opaque, releasing a sweet, briny aroma.

## Ingredients

(Makes 2 servings)

- 300 g raw peeled, deveined shrimp
- 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 2 tbsp dry white wine
- 3 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1/2 tsp chili flakes
- Juice of 1/2 lemon
- 1 tbsp chopped flat-leaf parsley
- 1/4 tsp fine sea salt
- 1 tbsp whole-grain breadcrumbs
- 2 cups arugula

## Method

1. Step 1: Pat the shrimp dry with paper towels and season with the salt. Arrange the arugula across two serving plates, then set the shrimp in a single layer over the greens.
2. Step 2: Heat the olive oil in a wide skillet over medium-low heat. Add the sliced garlic and chili flakes; cook for 45-60 seconds until the garlic smells fragrant and turns pale gold, watching closely because it browns quickly.
3. Step 3: Increase the heat to medium-high and add the shrimp in one layer. Cook for 60-90 seconds per side, until the exterior turns pink and the thickest pieces are just opaque throughout.
4. Step 4: Pour in the wine and scrape the browned garlic from the pan. Simmer for 2-3 minutes until the liquid reduces by about half and the shrimp are fully cooked, with no translucent centers.
5. Step 5: Remove the pan from the heat. Stir in the lemon juice and parsley, then spoon the sizzling garlic oil over the shrimp and arugula. Scatter with breadcrumbs and serve immediately.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 428 kcal
- Protein: 42 g
- Carbohydrates: 14 g
- Fat: 19 g
- Fiber: 3 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

For a more traditional presentation, reserve the shrimp shells, briefly toast them in the oil before removing them, and return them to the pan for the final minute; the shells add a deeper seafood aroma and crisp texture. If the shrimp are large, split them lengthwise before cooking so the centers cook evenly. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and reheat gently in a covered pan for 3-4 minutes, adding a splash of water if the garlic becomes too sharp.
